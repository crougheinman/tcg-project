# Entity Duel — The Borrowed Light

> *"It hums with borrowed light."* — Aether Well

Every card in this game draws on a Well, and every Well says the same thing about itself: the
light is **borrowed**. Borrowed implies a lender. Borrowed implies a debt. The whole world is
built on that single word, and each of the eight decks is a different answer to the same
question:

**Who is owed, and who pays?**

This document is the fiction behind the mechanics. Wherever possible the rules *are* the story —
a deck's curve, its keywords and its triggers are how its creed behaves under pressure. Card
names and existing flavour lines are treated as canon and built around, never contradicted.

---

## I. The Sundering, and what was left behind

Before the clans, there was one living thing large enough to be mistaken for a country. It had no
name, because nothing existed beside it to need one. It slept, and while it slept it dreamed, and
the dream was the world: weather, seasons, the green.

Something killed it. Nobody agrees what — the Treants say it simply finished, the Necromancers
say it was murdered, the Shogunate says the question is impolite.

It did not die cleanly. It died the way a great fire dies: slowly, in embers, for centuries. The
**Aether Wells** are those embers. Each one is a puncture in the buried body of the Sleeper, and
what rises through them is not water and not light but something in between — the last warmth of
a dream still going cold.

Tapping a Well is painless. That is the trap. You feel nothing, the mana comes, the spell lands.
What you have actually done is take a little more of a finite, dying warmth and spend it on
yourself. The Wells do not refill. They dim. A land tapped for mana untaps next turn because
*you* recovered — not because the Well did.

This is the Borrowed Light. Everyone in the world is drawing on the same slowly-emptying account,
and the eight great traditions differ only in what they think the right response to that is.

### The Hollowing

Where too many Wells have been drawn too hard, the land goes **hollow**: colour drains, sound
arrives a half-second late, and the dead do not settle. Hollowed ground is where the Grave Tide
is strongest, where the Treants will not walk, and where the Shogunate posts its quietest
garrisons.

Every battlefield in this game is hollowing ground. That is why both duellists can draw from the
same soil, and why the duel matters: the loser's Wells go to the winner's account.

## II. The Eight Answers

| Tradition | Creed | How it plays, and why |
|---|---|---|
| **Emberwood** | *Spend it fast and die young* | Haste, burn, few lands — the debt can't collect what's already gone |
| **Wildblitz** | *The wild repays what we take* | Swarm, anthems, lowest land count — many small lives, freely given |
| **Wildwood Titans** | *The forest is the lender* | Ramp, highest land count, giants — they aren't borrowing, they're being handed it |
| **Skyward** | *Never stand where the debt is collected* | Flyers over patient walls — evasion as a moral position |
| **Pyromancer** | *Burn the ledger* | Spell triggers, reach — every casting consumes a little of the record |
| **Stonewall** | *Owe nothing; outlast the collector* | Defender, lifegain, removal — refusal as a strategy |
| **Iron Blossom** | *A debt is settled in one cut* | Haste, instants, precision — pay in full, immediately, with honour |
| **Grave Tide** | *The dead already paid. Make them pay again* | Recursion, drain — reusing settled debts |

## III. Emberwood — *spend it fast*

**Region.** The scrub-and-ash country on the dying side of the Thornwood, where the Wells run hot
and shallow. An Emberwood Well gives generously and empties in a generation. The people there
have watched three villages go hollow in living memory and have drawn the obvious conclusion.

**Creed.** You cannot be billed for what you have already spent. Emberwood does not save, does not
fortify, does not plan past the season. It takes everything the Well will give and throws it at
the horizon while the giving is good. Outsiders call this recklessness. Emberwood calls it
honesty: everyone is spending the same dying warmth, and only Emberwood admits it out loud.

**In play.** Twelve lands against a curve that is almost entirely one to three, with two Dread
Maws as the only concession to a long game they do not expect to see. *Haste* everywhere — Ember Sprite
and Swift Lancer hit the turn they arrive, because a creature that waits is a creature taxed.
Cinderbolt and Scorch finish what the charge starts. Emberwood wins on turn seven or not at all,
and it knows it.

### Faces of Emberwood

**Kasei, the Whistling Girl** — *Kunoichi · "One whistle, and the wild answers."*
Orphaned by the first hollowing at Red Shallows, Kasei was raised by the animals that fled it
with her. She does not command beasts; she *reminds* them of something. Her whistle is a fragment
of the Sleeper's dream, and the three who always answer it — the lancer's mount, the talon on the
wind, the cub from the stone country — grow bolder for hearing it. (*Beast Blitz: Swift Lancer,
Sky Talon and Stoneback Cub get +1/+1 — exactly the three who come when she calls.*)

Kasei is the single most argued-over person in the world: Wildblitz claims her as a prophet,
Emberwood as a daughter, and the Treants have twice sent envoys asking her, politely, to stop.

**Rin of the Second Charge** — *Swift Lancer · "First to the charge, and gone before the dust settles."*
Rin has survived forty-one charges by never being present for the consequences of the first forty.
She is not brave. She has simply calculated, correctly, that the safest place in a battle is
ahead of it.

**The Dread Maw** — *"It does not hunt. It simply arrives, and the hunting is done."*
Not a creature of Emberwood. Not a creature of anywhere. The Maw surfaces wherever the hollowing
has gone deepest, and five of the eight traditions have learned to fight alongside one rather than
die explaining why they shouldn't. It is the clearest evidence anyone has that the Sleeper is not
entirely finished dreaming — and that the dream has begun to turn.

## IV. Wildblitz — *the wild repays*

**Region.** The same frontier as Emberwood, one valley north, and a doctrinal schism away.

**Creed.** Wildblitz agrees the debt is real and disagrees about who should carry it. Their answer
is the pack: no single life is worth much, but a hundred small lives freely given are worth more
than one hoarded great one. They run the fewest Wells of any tradition — ten — because they have
decided, as a matter of faith, to take as little as they can and make up the difference in bodies.

**In play.** Ten lands and a curve that tops out at **two** — the shallowest in the game. Call
Wolves for bodies, Wild Growth to
make one of them briefly matter, and Kasei's anthem over all of it. Wildblitz floods the board
and dares you to have enough blockers. Every creature in the deck is expendable and none of them
believe that about themselves.

### Faces of Wildblitz

**The Greyward Pack** — *Wolf tokens · "The pack answers the call."*
Not summoned. Invited. The Greyward have run the frontier since before the clans and come when
called because an older agreement says they should. Each wolf that falls is replaced by one that
was already coming.

**Old Bramm** — *Stoneback Cub · "Its hide remembers every mountain it has slept against."*
The cub is not a cub. Stonebacks are called cubs their whole lives because the alternative is
admitting how large they get. Bramm has slept against six mountains, and the pattern of his hide
is a map of them, which is why four separate traditions keep one near their lines: a stoneback
remembers where the ground is still whole.

## V. Wildwood Titans — *the forest is the lender*

**Region.** The deep Thornwood, where the Wells are not punctures but roots — the only places the
Sleeper's body remains more or less intact.

**Creed.** The Titans do not consider themselves borrowers. The forest grew out of the Sleeper and
so, in their reading, *the forest is the estate* — and an estate may spend its own inheritance.
This is either the most legitimate claim in the world or the most self-serving, depending on which
of the other seven you ask.

**In play.** Fourteen lands, the most of any tradition, and Overgrowth to put down two more at
once. They are not being frugal; they are being *given* it. What that mana buys is the heaviest
roster in the game: Ancient Treants at seven-seven, Dread Maws, Storm Drakes. Wildwood loses early
games and has never once lost a long one.

### Faces of the Wildwood

**The Elder Standing at Ellemar** — *Ancient Treant · "Older than the kingdoms that fear it."*
Remembers the Sundering as a change in the weather. Has been asked, by every tradition in turn,
what killed the Sleeper. Has answered every time, at length, in a language that takes four years
to say anything, and no one has yet stayed to hear the end of it.

**Thornwood Brute** — *"The forest walks when the forest is angry."*
Not a person. A mood. Brutes condense out of the Thornwood when the forest's patience fails and
dissolve back into it afterward; the lash of green that comes with one arriving is the forest
clearing its throat. (*Whipflash — when it enters, deal 1 damage to a creature.*) Emberwood keeps
exactly one, as a warning, and the Wildwood sends it.

## VI. Skyward — *never stand where the debt is collected*

**Region.** The cliff-holds and thermal roosts above the hollowing line. Nothing hollows at
altitude — that is the entire basis of Skyward's civilisation and the whole of its politics.

**Creed.** The debt is collected from the **ground**. Skyward's response is not to pay it, argue
it or outlast it, but to be somewhere else when it comes due. Their critics call this cowardice.
Skyward calls it the only tradition that has not lost a single settlement to the hollowing in two
hundred years, which is true, and which is why nobody enjoys arguing with them.

**In play.** The deck's shape is its argument. Six Stoneback Cubs and three Granite Sentinels
hold the ground — patiently, immovably, *expendably* — while two Sky Talons and two Storm Drakes
do the actual winning overhead. Skyward does not need its walls to triumph. It needs them to last
long enough that the sky can.

### Faces of Skyward

**Warden Ysolde of the Upper Hold** — *Granite Sentinel · "Patient as the mountain, immovable as its roots."*
Commands the ground garrison and has never been aloft. Skyward's wardens are chosen from families
who cannot fly, and are told this is an honour. Ysolde has come to believe it. Her sentinels hold
the cliff bases so the roosts above are never contested, and she has made peace with being the
part of her civilisation that is spent rather than saved.

**The Talon-Keeper, Ferrik** — *Sky Talon · "It hunts the wind itself."*
Keeps the roosts, and keeps the count. Ferrik's ledger of Well-depths is the most accurate in the
world, and he has shown it to no one, because what it predicts is that altitude will stop being
enough in roughly ninety years.

**The Storm Drake Vael** — *"Thunder is merely the sound of its wings."*
Drakes are not bred, bound or owned; they roost near the holds because the holds are warm. Vael
has fought beside Skyward, the Wildwood and Stonewall in a single season and considers none of
them allies. The ancient agreement is simpler than any treaty: the drakes keep the high air clear,
and in exchange nobody builds higher.

## VII. Pyromancer — *burn the ledger*

**Region.** No region. The Pyre is a school, and its adepts are found in all eight.

**Creed.** The most radical reading in the world: the debt is not a quantity but a *record*, and a
record can be destroyed. Every spell cast through a Pyre Adept consumes a sliver of that record
along with the mana. Cast enough, and — the school teaches — the account cannot be collected
because it can no longer be read.

Nobody outside the Pyre believes this. The Pyre's answer is that nobody outside the Pyre has tried.

**In play.** Four Pyre Adepts and a deck built to cast, cast, cast. Each sorcery through an Adept
costs the opponent a life directly — the ledger burning, in the fiction; reach, in the rules. Low
on bodies, heavy on Cinderbolt, Scorch and Pyroblast. Pyromancer is the only tradition that can
win without its creatures ever attacking.

### Faces of the Pyre

**Adept Sorae** — *Pyre Adept · "Every spell feeds the fire within."*
The school's most promising student and its most dangerous argument. Sorae can feel the record
thinning when she casts. She has never been able to demonstrate this to anyone, and has stopped
trying; she now simply casts more, faster, and the Pyre has quietly stopped asking her to explain.

**Her brother, Sael — see *Grave Tide*.**
They trained together. They reached opposite conclusions about the same evidence. This is the
single most important fact in the world's politics, and almost nobody knows it.

## VIII. Stonewall — *owe nothing*

**Region.** The curtain-forts along the hollowing line, built across the only three passes where
the hollow ground meets the whole.

**Creed.** The purest refusal in the world. Stonewall taps its Wells as little as it can, builds
instead of spends, heals instead of trades, and intends to still be standing when the account is
finally called in. They are not trying to win the argument about the debt. They are trying to
outlive everyone who is having it.

**In play.** Thirteen Wells and almost nothing to spend them on — because the mana is *held*, not
used. Bulwark cannot attack and is not meant to. Granite Sentinels wall the front four deep.
Soothe undoes damage; Topple drops the small and fast on their faces; Take Counter punishes the
one creature that over-commits. Stonewall closes with a lone Storm Drake, which is less a plan
than an admission that eventually even refusal has to end something.

### Faces of Stonewall

**Castellan Marrek** — *Bulwark · "The line holds here."*
Has held the middle pass for thirty-one years and has never once advanced. Marrek's standing order
is a single sentence, given to every recruit: *we are not here to win, we are here to still be
here.* He has buried four hundred soldiers under it, and he has not lost the pass.

**Sister Anwe of the Quiet House** — *Soothe · "Rest now. The walls will hold."*
Stonewall's healers do not use the Wells. Anwe's order holds that healing drawn from borrowed
light merely moves the debt from body to ledger — so they heal the slow way, with water, time and
a voice, and a Stonewall army recovers between assaults rather than during them.

**The Counter-Stroke** — *Take Counter · "Strike when its guard is down."*
A discipline, not a person: the trained patience to stand still while a thing commits, and kill it
in the moment it cannot withdraw. Stonewall teaches it as the only offensive act compatible with
the creed — nothing is spent, because the enemy spent it first.
*(Grave Tide has arrived at precisely the same tactic from precisely the opposite direction.
See §X.)*

## IX. Iron Blossom — *one cut, one debt, settled*

**Region.** The terraced valleys of the Blossom Clans, the only culture to have mapped every Well
it owns and the only one to have published the map.

**Creed.** A debt is not shameful. An *unsettled* debt is. The Clans tap freely and pay
immediately: every spell, every charge, every death is accounted for the same day it is incurred.
Their whole martial philosophy follows from their bookkeeping — strike once, strike completely, do
not leave a thing half-done to be finished later at interest.

**In play.** No Clan fights beside a Dread Maw, a drake or a wolf. Four Ashigaru, four Ronin
Blades, three Kensei Duelists and a single Iron Shogun, nearly all with *haste* — the Clans do not
hold troops in reserve, because a reserve is a debt to tomorrow. Their instants are the creed in
miniature: Iaijutsu Strike and Warding Stance resolve a fight in the instant it happens rather
than the turn after.

The roster is closed but for exactly one card. Of everything the Clans field, only **Insight** —
*draw two cards* — is shared with the outside world, and they share it with the three traditions
that think hardest: Skyward, the Pyre and Stonewall. The clan that published the Well-map holds
nothing in common with anyone except the means of knowing. That is not an accident of
deckbuilding. It is the whole of Tadeo's politics in one card.

### Faces of Iron Blossom

**The Iron Shogun, Tadeo Kurosa** — *"Where his banner rides, the line follows."*
The only Shogun in nine generations to ride at the front, which is doctrine, not bravado: a
commander who sends others to pay a debt he incurred has not settled it. (*Haste. The charge is
led from the front.*) Tadeo has 'published the map' — he is the one who ordered every Clan Well
surveyed and the depths made public, over the objection of every other tradition, because a debt
you cannot see is a debt you will not pay.

**Hana of the Single Breath** — *Kensei Duelist · "One breath, one cut. There is no second."*
The finest blade of her generation, and the strictest reading of the creed: she will not strike
twice at the same opponent. If the first cut does not settle it, she considers the account closed
and walks away. She has walked away four times. All four are still alive, and all four regard it
as the worst thing that has ever happened to them.

**Juro, the Masterless** — *Ronin Blade · "A masterless sword, restless even in the fray."*
Rode under Tadeo's banner at the Kettle and broke from it there — not from cowardice but from
arithmetic. The charge, he judged, would cost more lives than the ground was worth, and the creed
says you do not incur what you cannot settle. He was right. Tadeo knows he was right. Neither has
found a way to say so, and the Clans field four Ronin Blades to every one Shogun, which is its own
kind of verdict.

## X. Grave Tide — *the dead already paid*

**Region.** The hollowed flats, where nothing settles — including the buried.

**Creed.** The most quietly logical answer, and the one everyone finds unbearable. The debt is
paid by the living, out of their lives. The dead have already paid in full. Therefore the dead are
the only *solvent* labour in the world, and raising them does not deepen the account — it reuses a
settled one.

The Grave Tide does not consider itself evil. It considers itself the only tradition that has
read the terms.

### In play

The mechanics are the theology. **Plague Priest** drains a life with every sorcery — the rot
spreading. **Grave Necromancer** raises a Zombie on *any* spell, instants included, because the
dead do not care what kind of magic woke them. **Raise Horde** and the Zombie's own line — *"It
rises again, and again, and again"* — are the creed stated plainly. **Soul Siphon** turns an
enemy's last breath into your first. And **Grasp from the Grave** is the ground itself collecting:
cold hands closing on the ankles of something that overcommitted.

That last one is worth sitting with. Stonewall's Take Counter and Grave Tide's Grasp from the
Grave are *mechanically identical* — two mana, cast only while blocking, destroy the tapped thing
that just attacked. Two traditions that agree on nothing have independently arrived at the same
tactic: **let it commit, then take it.** One calls it discipline. The other calls it the harvest.

### Faces of the Tide

**Sael, First of the Read Terms** — *Grave Necromancer · "The dead make patient students."*
Trained at the Pyre beside his sister Sorae. They were given the same proof and the same ledgers.
Sorae concluded the record could be burned. Sael concluded the record was *correct*, and that the
only honest move left was to stop drawing on the living account entirely.

He is not a fanatic and has never claimed to be right about anything except the arithmetic. He
writes to Sorae twice a year. She has never opened one, and has never thrown one away.

**Mother Vess** — *Plague Priest · "Every incantation spreads the rot a little further."*
Does not raise the dead; she *prepares* the living, easing them across so that what rises is whole
enough to remember itself. The Tide's ghouls shamble and never tire because Vess made them
patiently. She regards the Shogunate's published Well-map as the first honest thing another
tradition has ever done, and has said so, which has made her unwelcome in her own flats.

**The Carrion Colossus** — *"The tide of the dead, made flesh at last."*
Not raised by anyone. When enough of the hollowed dead lie close enough together for long enough,
they begin to agree. The Colossus is what that agreement looks like standing up. Sael has seen
three. He did not summon any of them, and he has stopped telling people that, because nobody
believes him and the truth is worse.

## XI. Threads across the world

**The siblings.** Sorae (Pyromancer) and Sael (Grave Tide) are the world's central tragedy: same
teacher, same evidence, opposite conclusions, and both of them almost certainly partly right.
Every Pyre/Tide duel is a family argument conducted with armies.

**The identical tactic.** Stonewall's Take Counter and Grave Tide's Grasp from the Grave do the
same thing for opposite reasons. If you ever add a third block-only answer, it should come from a
tradition with a *third* reason — the pattern is the point.

**The shared beasts.** Stoneback Cubs appear in four traditions, Dread Maws in five, Cinderbolt in
five, Scorch and Insight in four each. These aren't filler — they are the world's commons.
Everyone fights with the same animals and the same fire because everyone is drawing on the same
dying warmth.

**The two that refuse the commons.** Grave Tide is the only roster that is *entirely* its own —
nine cards out of nine shared with nobody. Iron Blossom is one card away from the same, and that
card is Insight. The Tide excludes the world because it believes the world is insolvent; the Clans
exclude it because a debt held in common cannot be cleanly settled. Both arrive at isolation. Only
one of them left a door open, and what is behind the door is knowing.

**Kasei's three.** Swift Lancer, Sky Talon, Stoneback Cub — Emberwood, Skyward, and the commons.
Her whistle reaches across tradition lines, which is why she is claimed by two and feared by a
third.

**What nobody has.** No tradition has a card that *gives* anything back to a Well. That absence is
deliberate. The day someone designs one, it should be the most important card in the game.

## XII. Using this

The lore is written to be **harvested, not obeyed**:

- **Flavour text.** Characters here are attached to specific cards, so lines can be lifted
  straight into `src/cards/cards.ts`. Exactly four cards still have no `flavor` at all —
  **Cinderbolt, Scorch, Insight** and **Wild Growth** — and they are the obvious first candidates.
  Note that Insight is Iron Blossom's one shared card (§IX), so whatever it says has to read
  equally well in four traditions.
- **Deck descriptions.** The creed lines in §II are written to fit the `desc` field in
  `src/cards/decks.ts` at roughly the current length.
- **New cards.** Each tradition has explicit room: Skyward's ninety-year ledger, Stonewall's
  refusal to heal with Well-light, the Wildwood's unfinished answer, whatever the Pyre is actually
  doing to the record.
- **Balance is unaffected.** Nothing here asks for a rules change. Where the fiction and the rules
  disagree, the rules are canon and the fiction is wrong.
